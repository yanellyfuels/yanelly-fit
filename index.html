02 My Story

The reason I chose nutrition

My experience with hypothalamic amenorrhea is a big part of what led me toward nutrition.

I loved training, discipline, physique progress, and the feeling of doing something hard. Fitness gave me structure and confidence. Over time, though, my approach became less supportive and more stressful than I wanted to admit.

I lost my period and started noticing constant food thoughts, fatigue, feeling cold, mood changes, and the mental pressure of trying to keep a body that was not truly feeling well. Recovery has not been simple, and I have not fully recovered my menstrual cycle yet.

Some days I still mourn the smaller version of myself. At the same time, this experience is teaching me why fueling, rest, hormones, body image, and health cannot be separated from fitness goals. I am still learning, healing, and navigating this in real time.

03

You should not have to choose between loving fitness and protecting your health.

I do not believe women have to stop caring about strength, performance, muscle building, or physique goals. I do believe health has to come first. Fitness goals should not require sacrificing menstrual health, energy, mood, food freedom, or quality of life.

The goal is not to abandon fitness. The goal is to build a healthier relationship with fitness, food, recovery, and the body.

06 Food & Fueling

What fueling looks like for me

Food is still a joyful, practical, and visual part of Yanelly Fuels.

These are foods and meals I enjoy during this journey. I think about them as ways to increase variety, support satisfaction, fuel training, and care for overall nutrition. No single food cures HA or restores a cycle, and this is not a treatment plan.

Energy-dense staples

Hummus, avocado, butter, olive oil, nuts, and nut butters

Foods I am learning to include more comfortably for flavor, satisfaction, and a more supportive intake.

Protein and fats

Eggs, full-fat dairy, cottage cheese, yogurt bowls, and kefir

Simple foods that help meals feel more complete and make busy days easier to fuel.

Carbs I come back to

Rice, potatoes, bread, oats, fruit, and sweet potatoes

Carbs that support training, recovery, satisfaction, and meals that feel abundant instead of restricted.

07 Fitness

How I'm approaching movement right now

Strength training is still something I genuinely love.

My goal is not to completely abandon movement. I am learning to reduce unnecessary physical stress, prioritize rest and recovery, and let my training change when my health and guidance from qualified professionals call for it.

I still enjoy lower-body training, upper-body training, strength exercises, walking, mobility, and the feeling of getting stronger. I am also learning that more is not always better, and that recovery is part of the work.

Could You Be Underfueling?

Wondering if you might be underfueling?

Underfueling does not always look obvious. This free reflection guide was created for active women who want to better understand possible signs of low energy availability, reflect on their food and exercise habits, and feel more prepared to seek qualified support.

A gentle guide to recognizing possible signs of underfueling, reflecting on your relationship with food and exercise, and preparing questions to discuss with a qualified healthcare professional.

  • Changes in menstrual cycles
  • Energy, mood, sleep, and recovery
  • Constant food thoughts or increased food focus
  • Fear around eating more or exercising less
  • Training volume and rest
  • Possible next steps and questions for a healthcare professional
